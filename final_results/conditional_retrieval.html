Conditional Hybrid Retrieval

Research presentation

Does every query
need every retriever?

From Blended RAG to a learned cascade that spends neural computation only when it is likely to help.

A study of retrieval quality, recall budgets, and execution cost.

Ali Omar · Abdallah Mestrah · Abbas Yazbek
Supervisor: Dr. Ahmad Abboud · Lebanese University

Completed experimental scope · 21 October 2026. Based on the project manuscript and verified result reports.

Say: We started with the idea that some questions can use BM25 alone. Our project studies when that shortcut helps, how much quality it costs, and why a fast first stage is essential.

The starting point

Blended RAG improves the evidence
given to the generator.

Sawarkar, Mangal and Solanki (2024) study semantic indexes and hybrid queries across document fields.

Keyword search

BM25 finds exact words, names, and technical terms.

Dense search

Vector similarity retrieves passages with related meaning.

Learned sparse search

ELSER weights related vocabulary to support semantic matching.

The paper explores index and field-query combinations, including Best Fields, to improve retrieval and generated answers.

Source: Blended RAG, arXiv:2404.07220v2, sections III–V. Our three-way RRF baseline is an adaptation of this broader approach.

Say: The paper's central contribution concerns retrieval quality through semantic indexes and hybrid field queries. Avoid describing the original paper as exactly our BM25, MiniLM, SPLADE, and RRF code.

The gap we investigate

A stronger retrieval recipe still
leaves a cost question.

“Does this particular question need neural retrieval?”

The paper evaluates retrieval and answer quality across query strategies. It does not establish a learned BM25-first gate with a recall budget for each query.

Our static adaptation executes BM25, Dense, and SPLADE for every query. Easy lexical matches may need less work.

The trade-off

Skipping a neural branch can save time, but BM25 may miss documents that semantic retrieval finds.

Our question: how much mean retrieval time can we save while retaining an explicit share of fusion recall?

Quality and latency must be measured together. A high early-exit count alone is insufficient.

Paper context: Blended RAG. Efficiency question and static baseline: project manuscript, sections 1–2.

Say: This is the gap our study addresses, rather than a claim that the original system is universally slow. We measure the cost in our implementation.

Say: The gate only uses query and BM25 information. It does not need the correct documents or neural rankings to decide. Both neural retrievers search the corpus when escalation occurs.

What we changed

Precompute term contributions in postings and retrieve the top scores with a partial sort.

We checked score and ranking agreement, then gave the same optimization to both fusion and cascade.

Indexing reduces the unavoidable cost. Routing then saves part of the remaining work.

TREC means from separate original/indexed runs. Warm retrieval only. Experiments 24, 25, and 30 verify scorer compatibility and saved-ranking agreement.

Say: The indexing change preserves measured retrieval outputs. Its speedup and the gate's speedup are distinct contributions. The old-versus-new ratios also include variation between runs.

Freeze the policy.
Measure the calls that actually run.

Train and calibrate on NFCorpus train only.Inspect NFCorpus dev as development evidence.

Transfer frozen weights and thresholds.SCIDOCS and paper-dataset evaluations do not retrain the gate.

Measure complete query paths and branch counts.Warm components, synchronize GPU timing, and randomize method order.

Score saved rankings independently.Compare all gates with fusion and each standalone retriever.

Conclusion

Conditional retrieval helps when
the first stage is cheap enough.

We demonstrated a measured execution improvement over our full-fusion baseline.

What we established

Real neural-call skipping, a compatible indexed BM25 scorer, and an explicit quality–latency curve with frozen learned policies.

What limits the claim

SPLADE alone is faster with higher recall than the primary TREC cascade. On SQuAD, BM25 alone already clears a 90% retention floor.

Next research step: test the approach in the original Elasticsearch/ELSER system, compare modern adaptive baselines, and evaluate generated answers. Full current NQ and HotpotQA remain pending.

Read the evidence: project manuscript · final results · all operating points · original paper

Say: Our contribution is an empirical engineering study. We support additional mean-time savings against matched fusion, while reporting stronger standalone choices and failed retention targets. These results do not establish preserved answer quality or superiority over the published Blended RAG system.
